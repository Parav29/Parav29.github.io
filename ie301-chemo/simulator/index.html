    The rule in symbols

    decide a_i (told arrival), s_i (chair slot) for every patient i buffer s_i = a_i + B_c, P(D_i ≤ B_c) = q for class c ∈ {pre-cleared, same-day} chairs patients in chairs at any time ≤ K nurses M·(setups + takedowns) + patients on the drip ≤ M·N closing s_i + L_i ≤ closing time (else booked into overtime, else standby) minimise w₁·wait + w₂·idle chair time + w₃·overtime + w₄·sent home (in the simulation)

    How the simulator builds a day

    1. Draw the patient list. Patients are drawn until booked chair time reaches the booked share of K × opening hours. Each gets an infusion length from the mix and a set of random numbers for every stage.
    2. Estimate Bc. A pilot of 24 separate days is simulated under the rule, the realised delay D = ready − told is collected per class, and Bc is set to its q-quantile. This repeats twice, so registration and pharmacy queues caused by the rule itself are inside the buffer.
    3. Build the template. Patients are placed in template order on a 5-minute grid at the earliest time ≥ opening + Bc where a chair is free and the nurse limit holds through setup, drip and takedown (Hesaraki 2019). Told arrival = slot − Bc, rounded down to 15 minutes.
    4. Play the day. Arrivals deviate from the told time. Same-day patients go through registration, blood draw, lab, doctor review (some go home unfit), billing and pharmacy. Pre-cleared patients skip steps 2–4.
    5. Dispatch chairs. When a chair and a nurse are free, the ready patient with the earliest slot starts (or FCFS / longest / shortest for the 8 am rules). Nurses take down finished drips first; a finished patient waiting for a nurse blocks the chair.

    KPIs and how they are measured

    • Wait: actual arrival to start of drip, mean and 90th percentile, per class and per infusion length (equity).
    • Idle chair-hours inside opening hours, split into starvation (patients still in steps 1–6), nurse-bound (ready patient, no nurse) and no demand.
    • Overtime: last patient out minus closing time.
    • Not treated on booked day: unfit at review (pre-cleared ones are known the day before and never come in), sent home for capacity, and no-shows.
    • Nurse saturation: hours in opening time when no nurse could start a new setup.
    • Chair blocking: minutes a finished patient sat waiting for a nurse to take the line down.
